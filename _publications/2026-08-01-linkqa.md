We propose LinkSyn, a novel framework that synthesizes LinkQA — a 50B-token dataset — from multiple seeds tightly connected by knowledge points along graph paths. Training Llama-3 8B on 40B tokens of LinkQA yields an 11.51% average improvement on MMLU and CMMLU.

Motivation
------

High-quality, diverse training data is scarce, and synthetic QA has not closed the gap. Single-seed synthesis inherits the generator's bias and produces narrow diversity. Entity-graph methods link documents through co-occurring entities, but an entity is a surface mention rather than what a document is *about*, so the resulting links lack semantic coherence and integrate knowledge weakly. Neither approach can steer difficulty, discipline, or knowledge popularity with any precision.

Our intuition: a QA item typically tests only a few **knowledge points** (KPs) — the concepts, principles, theorems and methods that constitute a discipline. A KP is therefore a far tighter proxy for the item than an entity is, which makes KP co-occurrence a much stronger logical link. Supporting this, 75.43% of QA items in our seed pool touch multiple KPs.

Method
------

**Knowledge-point graph.** A distilled Qwen2.5-14B extractor labels seeds with KPs; two-stage consolidation leaves 10M KPs. The graph places KPs as nodes with undirected edges for co-occurrence, weighted by co-occurrence count — 10M nodes and 153M edges, with a giant component covering over 92% of texts. Community detection yields 21,806 clusters whose dominant subject averages 86.76% of content, confirming the graph organizes knowledge coherently.

**Knowledge value function.** Random walks over the graph generate KP paths, but two objectives compete: *coverage* favours the uniform distribution over KPs, *popularity* favours the empirical one. We define a knowledge distribution value balancing the two and prove that for squared Euclidean distance and reverse KL the optimum is their convex mixture. In practice paths are sampled under each policy and blended.

**Attribute-guided sampling.** Disciplines follow a 62-category national standard; difficulty uses five tiers anchored to estimated pass rates for top-university students under timed conditions. The difficulty scorer reaches Pearson r = 0.92 against human performance (Krippendorff's α = 0.85 across five PhD raters). Because raw seeds skew easy — roughly half at the lowest tier — sampling deliberately targets a harder distribution.

**Diffusion-based synthesis.** For each path, DeepSeek-R1 synthesizes QA from the multiple seeds strung along it, and DeepSeek-V3 refines the answers. Cleaning applies embedding-similarity and 10-gram decontamination against evaluation benchmarks.

Experiments
------

LinkQA is a general-domain multi-disciplinary corpus: 30B tokens plain, 50B tokens with CoT. Evaluation continues pretraining of Llama-3 8B from its 2T-token checkpoint on 40B tokens, mixing LinkQA 1:1 with KnowEdu, across 12 benchmarks. Baselines include general corpora (FineWeb-Edu, KnowEdu) and QA blends (Nemotron-CC, YulanQA, Nemotron-MIND, MegaMathQA, JiuZhang3.0).

| | MMLU | CMMLU | C-Eval | MMLU-STEM | AVG (12) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-training baseline | 55.08 | 52.23 | 57.11 | 45.17 | 43.12 |
| KnowEdu | 58.17 | 62.99 | 61.98 | 49.16 | 44.72 |
| JiuZhang3.0 | 56.55 | 60.30 | 59.52 | 48.68 | 48.01 |
| **LinkQA** | **63.98** | **66.35** | **65.59** | **56.95** | **49.57** |

The **11.51% average gain on MMLU and CMMLU** is measured against the pretraining baseline, alongside +6.45% across all 12 benchmarks and +1.56% over the strongest competing QA corpus. Gains hold at 1.7B, 8B and 16B parameters and from both 2T and 10T initial checkpoints. Ablations support the design: the balanced mixing weight beats either pure policy, and longer walks outperform shorter ones.

Insights
------

- **Multi-seed synthesis genuinely fuses seeds rather than paraphrasing one.** With graph-guided pairing, the gap between a synthesized item's maximum and minimum similarity to its seeds is 0.04–0.06, against 0.07–0.11 for randomly paired seeds — random pairing leaves outputs tethered to a single seed, while KP-linked seeds actually combine.
- **Coverage and popularity help different capabilities.** Tuning toward coverage aids knowledge-heavy benchmarks like MMLU and C-Eval; tuning toward popularity aids reasoning tasks such as ARC-C and DROP. The balanced mixture is best overall, but the split is informative about what each objective buys.
- **Graph structure raises difficulty for free.** LinkQA holds roughly 10× more high-difficulty items than Nemotron-CC, and synthesizing from two or three seeds adds about 10% more hard items than single-seed synthesis.
- **The advantage widens with scale.** Training loss falls faster and stays lower, with the gap to baselines growing visibly by 20B tokens.

**Limitations.** Without CoT, LinkQA trails on GSM8K and MATH. Target difficulty can only be approximated because high-difficulty seeds are scarce, and correctness declines as difficulty rises. Longer walks raise synthesis cost, forcing a diversity–efficiency trade-off. General-subject KPs bridge into other fields, so discipline shares drift away from the seed distribution.
