We propose FIRE, an extensible framework for comprehensive data quality assessment across multiple dimensions. FIRE aligns many quality signals into a unified space and integrates the characteristics of diverse data quality raters, producing a comprehensive quality signal for every data point. Experiments show FIRE outperforms other data selection methods, reaching target performance with less than 37.5% of the training data required by the random baseline.

Motivation
------

Data quality is one of the few remaining levers as scaling laws press against data and compute ceilings. But existing selection methods judge quality along a single axis — heuristic filters clean surface form without reading content, proximity methods resample toward Wikipedia or Books, and LLM-prompted raters each check one criterion. None delivers a comprehensive assessment.

The obvious fix fails in an instructive way. Packing every criterion into one prompt overwhelms the rater: our analysis shows single-rule GPT-4 judgments track human annotators well (ρ ≈ 0.77–0.86), but under a combined prompt the correlation for "required expertise" collapses to 0.32 and the reported share of high-quality text swings from 91.8% to 50.3%. Multi-dimensional raters exist, but how to *integrate* them has not been addressed systematically. That integration problem is what FIRE solves.

Method
------

Any rater qualifies if it emits a scalar score per document and has shown empirical usefulness. FIRE then proceeds in two stages.

**Rating alignment.** Raters disagree on both scale and on where "high quality" begins, so their scores are not directly comparable. We sample a reference subset matching the corpus quality distribution, rank the corpus by each rater and split it into intervals, then use GPT-4o to compute the win rate of each interval against the reference. Interpolating these gives a continuous win-rate-to-percentile mapping that places every rater on a common scale. The procedure is cheap — 20K comparisons per rater — and validated against human annotators at a Pearson correlation of 0.99. A useful byproduct: the win rate of a rater's top bucket serves as its **intrinsic reliability**.

**Rater integration.** Each rater spans a direction in quality space, but raters correlate, so naive summation double-counts overlapping dimensions. We define pairwise orthogonality from correlation, build a complete orthogonality graph over the raters, and show that overall orthogonality equals weighted degree centrality. A PageRank-style iteration propagates second-order effects, with convergence guaranteed via Perron–Frobenius. The final rating weights each aligned score by both its orthogonality and its intrinsic reliability; fully correlated raters collapse into one.

**Progressive selection.** Orthogonality is not constant across the quality range — top-ranked data reflects rater intent while the tail carries noise. Rather than one global top-k cut, FIRE Progressive repeatedly narrows the candidate pool, re-partitions it, and recomputes orthogonality locally.

Experiments
------

Selection runs over 627B SlimPajama tokens, training a 1.3B model on ≈20B tokens and a 3B model on 200B tokens, with four QuRating raters (writing style, facts and trivia, educational value, required expertise). Baselines span Random, DSIR, Density, ASK-LLM, each single rater, and four integration strategies — comprehensive rater, max criteria, average, mix criteria.

| 1.3B / 20B tokens | AVG (8 tasks) |
|---|---|
| Random | 47.5 |
| Best single rater (QuRating F.T.) | 48.1 |
| Best integration baseline (max criteria) | 47.9 |
| FIRE (4 raters) | 50.0 |
| **FIRE Progressive** | **50.4** |

FIRE gains **2.9% over random** and up to 1.9% over the strongest single rater, and reaches random's target performance on **less than 37.5% of the data**. Gains scale with the number of raters (48.4 → 49.6 → 50.0 for two, three and four). At 3B and 200B tokens, FIRE scores 55.7 against random's 54.1 — **+1.6% at equal FLOPs**. Compute cost matches QuRating's mix-of-criteria baseline.

Insights
------

- **Alignment is the load-bearing component.** Removing it costs 3.3% — dropping FIRE *below* plain averaging. Cross-rater comparability is the foundation everything else rests on; orthogonality contributes 2% and intrinsic reliability 0.2%.
- **Selected data is good everywhere, not spiked on one axis.** In a GPT-4o multi-dimension audit FIRE scores highest on the comprehensive dimension (99.5) while remaining strong on each individual one — exactly the behaviour single-rater selection fails to produce.
- **Quality and diversity need not trade off.** Embedding visualizations show single-rater selections clustering tightly while FIRE's spread far wider, suggesting integration preserves diversity rather than narrowing the corpus toward one rater's taste.
- **More raters keep helping.** The monotone improvement from two to four raters, plus a successful run on an entirely different rater set, indicates the framework is genuinely extensible rather than tuned to one combination.

**Limitations.** The orthogonality weighting assumes linear additivity, and experiments cover four raters.
